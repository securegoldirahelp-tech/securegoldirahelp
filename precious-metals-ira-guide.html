Precious Metals IRA: What Every Retirement Investor Should Know

A plain-language guide to how these accounts work, what metals qualify, what fees to expect, and how to think through whether this fits your retirement picture.

What Is a Precious Metals IRA?

Most retirement accounts — the kind you get through an employer or open at a brokerage — hold stocks, bonds, and mutual funds. That is a standard IRA, and the custodian typically limits you to those conventional investments.

A self-directed IRA works differently. It follows the same IRS rules as any traditional or Roth IRA — the same contribution limits, the same tax treatment — but it allows a broader range of assets, including physical precious metals.

A Precious Metals IRA is a self-directed IRA that holds physical gold, silver, platinum, or palladium — coins or bars that meet IRS purity standards. The metals are not stored at your home; they sit in an approved depository on your behalf.

A Gold IRA is the most common version — a Precious Metals IRA focused on gold. The two terms are often used interchangeably, though technically a Precious Metals IRA can hold all four approved metals.

The account is still an IRA. It has the same annual contribution limits and the same tax-deferred (or tax-free, for Roth) structure. What changes is what the IRA owns — physical metal rather than a fund or stock. For a deeper look at how the rollover process works, see our complete Gold IRA rollover guide.

What Metals Can Be Held in a Precious Metals IRA?

The IRS permits four metals inside a self-directed IRA, but only when they meet specific purity requirements. Not every coin or bar qualifies. Collectibles, rare coins, and jewelry generally do not meet the standard. Personal storage — keeping the metals at home — is not allowed regardless of metal type.

Gold
.995 fineness minimum

American Gold Eagles, American Gold Buffalo, Canadian Gold Maple Leaf, approved gold bullion bars from recognized refiners.

Silver
.999 fineness minimum

American Silver Eagles, Canadian Silver Maple Leaf, approved silver bullion bars from recognized mints and refiners.

Platinum
.9995 fineness minimum

American Platinum Eagles, Canadian Platinum Maple Leaf, approved platinum bullion bars and rounds.

Palladium
.9995 fineness minimum

American Palladium Eagles, Canadian Palladium Maple Leaf, approved palladium bullion bars.

Why Do Some Retirees Consider Precious Metals?

There is no single right answer to portfolio composition in retirement. What works for one person may not work for another. That said, here are the reasons retirees most often cite when exploring precious metals as part of a broader strategy:

  • Inflation concern. Gold has historically maintained purchasing power over long periods. For retirees on fixed income, retirement inflation protection is a recurring priority.
  • Diversification beyond stocks and bonds. Physical metals are a separate asset class that has not historically moved in lockstep with equities.
  • Currency exposure. Some investors view physical metals as a way to reduce reliance on the value of the dollar during periods of expanded money supply.
  • Tangibility. Unlike a stock or bond, physical metal is a real object. Some investors find that reassuring in uncertain times.
  • Portfolio balance. Precious metals may serve as a counterweight to heavy paper-asset concentration — not as a replacement, but as a complement.

It is equally worth noting what precious metals are not: they do not pay dividends or interest, they can be volatile in the short term, and they carry real costs. No outcome is guaranteed. The decision to include them in a retirement account involves trade-offs like any other investment choice.

How a Precious Metals IRA Works: A 5-Step Overview

Opening a Precious Metals IRA involves more steps than a conventional IRA. There are specialized custodians, metal dealers, and depositories involved. Here is how the process generally works:

1

Choose a Self-Directed IRA Custodian

Not every IRA custodian handles precious metals. You need a custodian specifically approved to administer self-directed IRAs holding physical assets. Do your due diligence on fees and reputation before selecting one.

2

Open the Account

Complete the custodian’s application, agree to their fee schedule, and establish the self-directed IRA. This is similar to opening any IRA, with additional paperwork around the alternative asset structure.

3

Transfer or Roll Over Retirement Funds

Fund the account via a direct transfer from an existing IRA, or by rolling over funds from an eligible employer plan such as a 401(k) to Gold IRA rollover. Properly structured rollovers avoid taxes and penalties. See which retirement accounts qualify for a full breakdown.

4

Select IRS-Approved Metals

Work with an approved dealer to choose the specific coins or bars you want to purchase. The metals must meet IRS purity standards (see Section 3 above). Your custodian will facilitate the purchase on behalf of your IRA.

5

Metals Are Sent to an Approved Depository

Important: IRA-owned metals must be stored at an IRS-approved, independent depository — not at your home, not in a personal safe, not anywhere you personally control. Taking personal possession of IRA metals before a qualified distribution is treated by the IRS as a taxable distribution.

Fees to Expect With a Precious Metals IRA

Precious Metals IRAs generally carry more fees than standard IRAs. Understanding the full cost structure upfront matters. The Gold IRA fees explained section of our rollover guide covers this in greater detail. Here is an overview of the categories to ask about:

Account Setup Fee

A one-time fee charged by the custodian to open the self-directed IRA. Ranges widely — from $0 to several hundred dollars depending on the provider.

Annual Custodian Fee

An ongoing administrative fee for maintaining the account. May be a flat rate or tied to the value of assets held. Ask whether this is flat or scaled.

Storage Fee

Charged by the depository for storing your metals. This is typically annual and may be flat or a percentage of metal value. Segregated storage often costs more than commingled.

Dealer Premium

When you purchase metals, the dealer charges a premium above the spot price. This is effectively the dealer’s markup and varies by metal type, form, and dealer.

Transaction Fees

Some custodians charge per-transaction fees when you buy or sell metals within the account. Ask about these before initiating any trades.

Wire / Transfer Fees

Fees for moving money in or out of the account — check for these before initiating a rollover or distribution.

Fee transparency is an important factor when evaluating any custodian or dealer. A provider that makes it difficult to get a clear, written fee schedule before you open an account warrants caution.

Risks and Things to Consider

A Precious Metals IRA is not a default upgrade from a conventional IRA — it is a different tool, with real trade-offs. Here is an honest look at the considerations:

  • Not every investor needs physical metals. A well-diversified conventional portfolio may already meet your retirement income needs. Adding precious metals is a choice, not a requirement.
  • Price volatility is real. Gold and silver prices can swing meaningfully in short periods. If you expect smooth, predictable returns, precious metals will sometimes disappoint.
  • Fees reduce net returns. Every fee you pay — setup, storage, custodian, dealer premium — is a cost that works against your total return. In a conventional IRA, many of these fees don’t exist at all.
  • No income generation. Unlike dividend stocks or bonds, physical metals do not pay interest or dividends. Your return depends entirely on price appreciation.
  • Liquidity is more involved. Selling physical metals inside an IRA requires coordinating with the custodian and dealer. It is not as instant as selling a stock.
  • RMD planning. Traditional Precious Metals IRAs are subject to Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) starting at age 73. Satisfying an RMD may require liquidating metals — plan ahead.
  • Context in your overall portfolio matters. Whether precious metals make sense depends on your existing asset mix, income needs, time horizon, and retirement goals.

A well-informed investor makes better decisions. Understanding the limitations of an account type is just as important as understanding its potential benefits.

Precious Metals IRA FAQ

Straightforward answers to the questions we hear most often — no sales language, no pressure.

What is the difference between a Gold IRA and a Precious Metals IRA?

A Precious Metals IRA is the broader category — a self-directed IRA that can hold IRS-approved gold, silver, platinum, or palladium. A Gold IRA is the most common type, focused specifically on gold. All Gold IRAs are Precious Metals IRAs, but not all Precious Metals IRAs hold only gold. In everyday conversation the terms are often used interchangeably.

Can I hold silver in a Precious Metals IRA?

Yes. Silver is one of the four metals the IRS permits inside a self-directed IRA. It must meet a minimum purity of .999 fineness. Common eligible options include American Silver Eagles and certain approved silver bullion bars. Not all silver products qualify — confirm with your custodian before purchasing.

Can I store Precious Metals IRA gold at home?

No. IRS rules require that metals held inside an IRA be stored at an approved, independent depository — not at your home, in a personal safe, or in a safety deposit box you control. Taking personal possession of IRA-owned metals is treated by the IRS as a taxable distribution and may also trigger early withdrawal penalties.

Do Precious Metals IRAs have higher fees?

Generally, yes. Precious Metals IRAs typically carry more fees than conventional IRAs — including setup fees, annual custodian fees, storage fees at the depository, and dealer premiums when metals are purchased. Understanding the complete fee structure before you open an account is an important part of evaluating whether this type of account makes sense for your situation.

What happens when I reach RMD age?

Traditional Precious Metals IRAs are subject to Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs) starting at age 73 — the same as traditional IRAs. To satisfy an RMD, you may need to liquidate a portion of the metals held in the account or take an in-kind distribution (receiving the physical metal rather than cash). RMD planning for a Precious Metals IRA is worth discussing with a qualified tax advisor before you reach that age.

Can Roth IRAs be converted into Precious Metals IRAs?

Yes. A self-directed Roth IRA can hold IRS-approved precious metals, following the same Roth tax rules — contributions are made with after-tax dollars, and qualified distributions are generally tax-free. A Roth conversion may have tax implications in the year it occurs. Consult a tax professional regarding your specific situation before converting.

Is a Precious Metals IRA right for everyone?

No — and that is worth saying plainly. A Precious Metals IRA makes sense for some investors and not for others. Factors like your overall retirement portfolio, time horizon, income needs, liquidity preferences, and existing diversification all matter. Because precious metals carry real fees and do not generate income, they work best as one part of a broader, thoughtfully constructed retirement strategy. This is a decision best made with a qualified financial advisor who knows your full picture.
