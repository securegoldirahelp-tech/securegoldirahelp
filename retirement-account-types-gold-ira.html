TSP, Pension, Annuity: Which Retirement Accounts Can Roll Into a Gold IRA?

IRA/401k Gold Retirement Guide — Plain-English Education

Before moving any retirement savings into a Gold IRA, one question matters above all others: does your current account actually qualify? The answer depends entirely on what type of account you hold — and the rules vary more than most people expect.

This guide walks through every major retirement account type, whether it can roll into a Gold IRA, and the key restrictions or conditions that apply. No jargon, no pressure — just a clear picture of where you stand.

Why Account Type Matters

A Gold IRA is a self-directed IRA that holds IRS-approved physical precious metals instead of stocks or mutual funds. Like any IRA, it has specific rules about which assets can fund it and how money can move in.

The IRS governs rollovers. Each retirement account type has its own rollover rules — some allow direct transfers, some require a 60-day indirect rollover, and some come with restrictions that catch people off guard. Getting this right from the start avoids unnecessary taxes and penalties.

Account-by-Account Breakdown

Traditional IRA

Eligible: Yes

A Traditional IRA rolls into a Gold IRA cleanly and directly. This is the most straightforward transfer available. You can move all or part of the balance, and no taxes are due as long as the funds go directly from custodian to custodian. There are no restrictions based on your employment status.

Roth IRA

Eligible: Yes — into a Roth Gold IRA

Roth IRA funds must move into a Roth-designated Gold IRA, not a Traditional one. The tax-free growth status is preserved as long as the account type matches. The mechanics are the same as a Traditional IRA transfer — direct, custodian to custodian.

401(k) — Traditional

Eligible: Yes, with one important condition

A Traditional 401(k) can roll into a Gold IRA — but only if you are no longer employed by the sponsoring employer. If you are still working for the company whose 401(k) you hold, the plan typically does not allow a rollover while you remain employed. There is one exception: some plans offer an “in-service distribution” for participants over age 59½. Check your plan documents before assuming you qualify. An old 401(k) from a previous employer has no employment restrictions.

401(k) — Roth

Eligible: Yes — into a Roth Gold IRA

The same employment rules apply as with a Traditional 401(k). Once eligible, Roth 401(k) funds must go into a Roth Gold IRA to preserve their after-tax status.

403(b)

Eligible: Yes

403(b) plans are common for teachers, healthcare workers, and nonprofit employees. Like a 401(k), a 403(b) can roll into a Gold IRA once you separate from your employer or, in some cases, after age 59½ through an in-service distribution. Traditional 403(b) funds roll into a Traditional Gold IRA; Roth 403(b) funds roll into a Roth Gold IRA.

457(b)

Eligible: Yes, with a distinction between plan types

Governmental 457(b) plans roll into a Gold IRA without penalty, even before age 59½. Non-governmental 457(b) plans — offered by some nonprofits — have more restrictions and may not be eligible for rollover into an IRA at all. If you are unsure which type you hold, contact your plan administrator.

TSP — Thrift Savings Plan

Eligible: Yes, but only after separation from federal service

The Thrift Savings Plan covers federal employees and military personnel. Active federal employees cannot roll TSP funds into a Gold IRA. Those who have separated from federal service — including retirees — can roll their TSP balance into a Traditional IRA, including a self-directed Gold IRA. The TSP’s Roth balance must go to a Roth-designated Gold IRA.

Pension Plans

Eligible: It depends on the payout structure

Traditional defined-benefit pensions typically pay out as a monthly annuity for life. Once a pension is in payout mode, those monthly payments cannot be rolled into a Gold IRA. However, if your pension offers a lump-sum distribution option and you have not yet elected a payout, that lump sum can be rolled into a Gold IRA. If you have already begun collecting pension payments, rollover is not available.

Annuities

Eligible: Sometimes — depends on the annuity type

Annuities held inside an IRA or qualified retirement plan (“qualified annuities”) can be rolled over, subject to the same rules as the account that holds them. Non-qualified annuities — funded with after-tax dollars outside a retirement account — generally cannot be rolled into a Gold IRA. Once an annuity is annuitized and paying out monthly, the same limitations as a pension apply.

SEP-IRA

Eligible: Yes

A SEP-IRA is treated like a Traditional IRA for rollover purposes. Self-employed individuals and small business owners can roll it directly into a Gold IRA with no tax consequences. There are no employment or age restrictions.

SIMPLE IRA

Eligible: Yes, with a two-year rule

A SIMPLE IRA can roll into a Gold IRA, but only after you have participated in the plan for at least two years. Before that two-year window closes, a distribution carries a 25% penalty rather than the usual 10%. After two years, a SIMPLE IRA moves just like a Traditional IRA.

The Most Common Points of Confusion

Still employed? Your current 401(k) may be off-limits.

Many people assume they can roll over their 401(k) at any time. If you are still working for the employer sponsoring the plan, you likely cannot — unless the plan allows in-service distributions after 59½. An old 401(k) from a previous employer has no such restriction.

TSP participants need to wait for separation.

Active federal employees cannot move TSP funds into a Gold IRA. After you leave federal service or retire, the rollover option opens.

Annuities vary widely.

Whether a rollover is possible depends on whether your annuity is qualified or non-qualified, and whether payments have already started. If you are unsure what kind of annuity you hold, contact the insurance company that issued it before making any decisions.
